# Experiment 5: Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

## Objectives

- **(i)** To determine the acceleration due to gravity, *g* using a simple pendulum. 
- **(ii)** To investigate the effect of large amplitude oscillation on the accuracy of acceleration due to gravity, *g* obtained from the experiment.

## Theory

An oscillation of a simple pendulum is an example of simple harmonic motion (SHM) if:

1. The mass of the spherical bob is a point mass
2. The mass of the string is negligible
3. The amplitude of the oscillation is small (< 10°)

According to the theory of SHM, the period of oscillation of a simple pendulum, *T* is given by:

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}} \tag{5.1}$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \text{where,} \quad
    l &= \text{length of pendulum} \\
    g &= \text{acceleration due to gravity} 
\end{aligned}
$$

Rearranging `equation 5.1`, we obtain:

$$T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{g}l \tag{5.2}$$

> A graph of $T^2$ against $l$ is a straight line with gradient equal to $\frac{4\pi^2}{g}$.

From the gradient of the graph, the acceleration due to gravity, *g* can be calculated using:

$$g = \frac{4\pi^2}{\text{gradient}}$$

For large amplitude oscillations (> 10°), the period deviates from the simple formula. This introduces systematic error that increases with amplitude, demonstrating the importance of the small amplitude condition for accurate results.

## Apparatus

- Piece of string (≈105 cm)
- Small pendulum bob
- Pair of small flat pieces of wood or cork
- Retort stand with a clamp
- Stopwatch
- Metre rule
- Protractor with a hole at the centre of the semicircle
- Optical pin
- Pair of scissors or a cutter
- Stabilizing weight or a G-clamp

## Procedure

### Part A: Small Amplitude Oscillations (< 10°)

#### Setup

1. Set up a simple pendulum as shown in **`Figure 5.1`**

#### Data Collection

2. Measure the length, *l* of the pendulum at **40 cm**
3. Release the pendulum at **less than 10°** from the vertical in one plane
4. Measure the time, *t* for **10 complete oscillations**
   > **Note:** Start the stopwatch after several complete oscillations to ensure steady motion
5. Calculate the average time, *t̄*
6. Calculate the period of oscillation, *T* using: $T = \frac{\bar{t}}{10}$
7. Repeat steps 2-6 for pendulum lengths: *l* = **50 cm, 60 cm, 70 cm, 80 cm, and 90 cm**

#### Data Analysis

8. Tabulate all the data with columns for length, time, and period
9. Calculate *T²* for each measurement
10. Plot a graph of *T²* against *l*
11. Determine the acceleration due to gravity, *g* from the gradient using:
    $$g = \frac{4\pi^2}{\text{gradient}}$$
12. Determine the uncertainty of acceleration due to gravity, *Δg*

### Part B: Large Amplitude Oscillations (≈ 70°)

#### Data Collection 

13. Fix the length, *l* of the pendulum at **100 cm**
14. Release the pendulum through a **large arc of about 70°** from the vertical
15. Measure the time, *t* for **5 complete oscillations**
16. Repeat the measurement **three times**
17. Calculate the average time, *t̄*
18. Calculate the period of oscillation, *T* using: $T = \frac{\bar{t}}{5}$

#### Calculation for Large Amplitude

19. Calculate the acceleration due to gravity, *g* using **equation 5.1** directly:
    $$g = \frac{4\pi^2 l}{T^2}$$
    
    using the length *l* = 100 cm and period *T* from step 18

### Comparison and Analysis

18. **Compare the results** from both parts:
    - *g* from small amplitude (Part A): .........  m/s²
    - *g* from large amplitude (Part B): .........  m/s²
    - Standard value: **9.81 m/s²**

19. Write detailed comments explaining:
    - The difference between the two values
    - The effect of amplitude on SHM assumptions
    - Sources of systematic error in each case
