# Experiment 6: Standing Waves

## Objectives

- (i) To investigate standing waves formed in a stretched string.
- (ii) To determine the mass per unit length, *µ* of the string.

## Theory

When a stretched string is vibrated at a frequency, *f* the standing waves formed have both ends as nodes. The frequency in the string obeys the following relation:

$$f = \frac{1}{2l}\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$$

Rearranging the equation, the tension of the string is:

$$T = 4f^2\mu l^2 \tag{6.1}$$

where,

$$
\begin{aligned}
    f &= \text{frequency of vibration} \\
    l &= \text{length between two nodes } \\
    T &= \text{tension in the string} \\
    \mu &= \text{mass per unit length} \\
\end{aligned}
$$

> A graph of $T$ against $l^2$ is a straight line with gradient equal to $4f^2\mu$.

From the gradient of the *T* vs *l²* graph, the mass per unit length, *µ* can be calculated using:

$$\mu = \frac{\text{gradient}}{4f^2}$$

## Apparatus

- G-clamp
- Solenoid (about 100 turns) or ticker timer
- AC power supply (2–4 V)
- Metal rod (soft iron)
- Two bar magnets
- Magnet holder
- Piece of string (approximately 2 m long)
- Pulley with clamp
- Wooden wedges
- Set of slotted masses: 2 g, 5 g, 10 g, and 20 g
- Metre rule
- Connecting wires
- Wooden block

## Procedure

1. Set up the apparatus as shown in `Figure 6.1`

  <img src="/labs/lab-06/figure-6-1.png" alt="Figure 6.1: Standing Waves Apparatus" style="max-width: 400px; margin: 20px 0;" />
</div>

2. Connect the terminals of the solenoid to the AC power supply (2 V, 50 Hz)
   > **Caution:** Do not exceed 4 V to avoid damage to the solenoid

3. Place the metal rod between the two bar magnets

4. Tie one end of the string to the rod and the other to the hook of the slotted mass
   > **Important:** Make sure that the length of the string from the end of the rod to the pulley is **not less than 1.5 m**

5. Clamp the metal rod properly
6. Switch on the power supply
7. Adjust the position of the metal rod to get **maximum vibration**

8. Place the wooden wedges below the string as **close as possible to the pulley**

9. Adjust the position of the wooden wedges until a clear **single loop standing wave (fundamental mode)** is observed

10. Record the distance, *l* between the wedges (**this is the vibrating length**)
11. Record the total mass, *m* (mass of the hook and the slotted mass)
12. Calculate the weight (tension): $W = mg$
    > **Note:** Weight, $W$ = Tension, $T$ 

13. Add a small mass, **preferably 10 g**, to the hook
14. Repeat steps 9-12 for **at least six different readings**

15. Tabulate all the data with columns for mass, tension, vibrating length, and length squared

16. Calculate *l²* for each measurement

17. Plot a graph of *T* (tension) against *l²*

18. Calculate the gradient from the best-fit line

19. Determine the mass per unit length, *µ* using:
    $$\mu = \frac{\text{gradient}}{4f^2}$$
    
    where *f* = 50 Hz (frequency of the AC supply)

20. Determine the uncertainty of mass per unit length, *Δµ*

21. Weigh the mass of the actual string
22. Measure the total length of the string
23. Calculate the actual mass per unit length, *µ* using:
    $$\mu = \frac{\text{Total mass of string}}{\text{Total length of string}}$$

24. Compare the mass per unit length obtained from:
    - Step 19 (from graph gradient) 
    - Step 23 (from direct measurement)
    
    Write comments explaining any differences
